Mathematical Foundation

How Yukti Encodes Any Bitstream

A direct digital adaptation of Pingala's Uddishta (ranking a pattern) and Nashta (recovering a pattern from its rank).

01
📊

Count Gurus (k)

Slice the raw bitstream into blocks of $n$ bits. Count the number of 1-bits (Gurus). Store $k \in [0, n]$ in $\lceil \log_2(n+1) \rceil$ bits.

k = popcount(block)
02
🔢

Find Lexicographic Rank

Using Pingala's Uddishta method, determine the exact index of this permutation within all $\binom{n}{k}$ possibilities. Store $\text{rank}$ in $\lceil \log_2 \binom{n}{k} \rceil$ bits.

rank = uddishta(block, k)
03
🛡️

Exact Lossless Recovery

Recovery applies Nashta: by subtracting $\binom{n-1-i}{\text{left}}$ whenever $\text{rank} \ge \binom{\cdot}{\cdot}$, the exact bit sequence is restored with zero loss.

block = nashta(n, k, rank)

Empirical Integrity

Does MeruCode Truly Compress?

A hackathon project must be honest. Here is our mathematical and empirical truth:

📉

When It Compresses

On density-skewed data (sparse or dense bitstreams where $p(1) \ll 0.5$ or $p(1) \gg 0.5$), $\log_2 \binom{n}{k} \ll n$. Compression ratios achieve 30% – 60% shrinkage.

⚖️

When It Breaks Even

On uniformly balanced data ($k \approx n/2$), entropy is maximized. The binomial layer size $\binom{n}{n/2} \approx 2^n / \sqrt{\pi n / 2}$. With block headers, the ratio is $\approx 1.05\times$. We never hide this.

🔬

Complementary to zlib

zlib (Deflate) searches for repeated byte strings (LZ77). Yukti exploits bit-level Hamming weight bias. They solve fundamentally different compression dimensions.

Judges' Honesty Pledge: Every benchmark test runs raw, zlib, and Yukti side-by-side on the exact same inputs without cherry-picking. All 1,000 cross-language parity vectors and 82 unit tests are publicly verifiable in source code.
